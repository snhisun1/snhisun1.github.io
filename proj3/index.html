Using the same starting noises, classifier-free guidance produces sharper faces and more consistent portrait compositions. The distorted figure from seed 100 becomes a recognizable portrait. B 1.2.3: Denoising Pure Noise

Fresh UNet with hidden dimension 128, batch size: 256, Adam learning rate: 0.0001, training: 5 epochs, inputs: independent Gaussian noise, targets: clean MNIST digits.

After both epochs 1 and 5, the outputs look like a blurry mix of digits rather than one clear digit. Furthermore, the bright shapes overlap around the center, where many of the training digits have strokes. This is because the input is random noise independent of the target image: it gives the model no information about which digit to predict. Also, with MSE loss, predicting an average of the training images reduces the error across different targets. This explains why the output resembles an "average" digit instead of a new, distinct digit.

B 2.6: Sampling and the No-Scheduler Experiment

To remove the scheduler, I used a constant learning rate of 0.002 instead of starting at 0.01 and gradually lowering it. To compensate for removing this decay, I chose a lower learning rate from the beginning rather than keeping the original starting rate of 0.01 for all 10 epochs. The rate of 0.002 is five times lower than that starting rate, but still higher than the scheduled model’s final rate of 0.001. This was intended to balance learning progress with smaller adjustments as the model improved. After 10 epochs, the model without the scheduler still produces recognizable digits and looks similar in quality to the model with the scheduler. Both use the same sampling seed, 300 Euler steps, and guidance scale of 5, so the sample grids can be compared directly. In this run, the lower constant learning rate maintained similar visual quality without needing a scheduler.
